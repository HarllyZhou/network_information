# Narrative

## Definition

A narrative is a prior about an economic state that an agent combines with data. In Flynn and Sastry the state is aggregate productivity. An optimistic narrative is the belief that productivity is high on average. A pessimistic narrative is the belief that it is low on average. A firm puts weight on one of these priors and updates with a private signal.

A single pair of narratives can be the whole model. A constellation is a larger set of granular narratives whose shares combine into one optimistic fraction.

## Evidence

- In the estimated model the gap between the optimistic and pessimistic means is 0.028, and the signal-to-noise ratio is 0.344. Optimistic firms, measured from 10-K language, hire 3.6 percentage points more than pessimistic firms in the same industry and year. That language predicts lower future stock returns and profitability. Source: [The Macroeconomics of Narratives](../sources/2024-06-01-flynn-sastry-macroeconomics-of-narratives.md).
- Moving the whole economy from pessimism to optimism changes log output by \(\alpha\delta^{OP}/(1-\omega)\). The partial-equilibrium hiring effect is \(\delta^{OP}\). The factor \(1/(1-\omega)\) is the demand multiplier. The calibration sets that multiplier at 1.96. Source: [The Macroeconomics of Narratives](../sources/2024-06-01-flynn-sastry-macroeconomics-of-narratives.md).
