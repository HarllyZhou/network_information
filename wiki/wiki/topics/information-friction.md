# Information friction

Exogenous signals, sticky information, fixed information capacity, elastic rational inattention, a switch between a decreasing gain and a constant gain, and a narrative prior combined with a private signal are entries here, not separate concepts.

## How it is modelled

- La'O and Tahbaz-Salehi. Firms set nominal prices from private signals about productivity and do not observe the aggregate state. The information structure is exogenous: signals may depend on the productivity shocks and not on prices. Sticky information, in which a fraction of firms see the shocks and the rest price from the prior, is a special case. Price flexibility \(\phi_i\) is a parameter. Source: [Optimal Monetary Policy in Production Networks](../sources/2022-05-01-lao-tahbaz-salehi-optimal-monetary-policy-production-networks.md).
- Fang, Huo, Li, and Wu. Firms are rationally inattentive with elastic capacity. Under a quadratic approximation they track nominal marginal cost at a constant marginal cost \(\chi_i\) of mutual information. If \(\chi_i\) is low enough relative to the benefit, the firm buys one noisy signal of marginal cost. Price flexibility is chosen, \(\mu_i = 1 - \chi_i/(\theta_i\lambda_i\mathrm{V}(\mathrm{mc}_{it}))\). A fixed information capacity leaves total information unchanged when marginal-cost variance rises. Source: [Inattentive Network](../sources/2025-06-16-fang-huo-li-wu-inattentive-network.md).
- Carvalho, Eusepi, Moench, and Preston. Firms know the short-run law of inflation and learn only its mean. They use a decreasing gain while a statistic built from past forecast errors stays below a threshold, and a constant gain \(g\) once it crosses. The first regime is [anchored](../concepts/inflation-expectation-anchoring.md). The second is unanchored. Source: [Anchored Inflation Expectations](../sources/2023-01-01-carvalho-eusepi-moench-preston-anchored-inflation-expectations.md).
- Flynn and Sastry. Firms hold a [narrative](../concepts/narrative.md), a prior over the mean of aggregate productivity, and observe a private Gaussian signal of the current fundamental. The signal-to-noise ratio is \(\kappa = 1/(1+\sigma_e^2/\sigma_\theta^2)\). At \(\kappa = 0\) the posterior that matters for hiring is the narrative. At \(\kappa = 1\) the narrative drops out of that posterior. The estimate is \(\kappa = 0.344\). Source: [The Macroeconomics of Narratives](../sources/2024-06-01-flynn-sastry-macroeconomics-of-narratives.md).

## Interaction with the real economy

- In La'O and Tahbaz-Salehi, incomplete information makes nominal prices respond only insofar as the shocks are in the firm's signal. On a given [production network](../concepts/production-network.md), a stickier supplier lowers the upstream flexibility of its customers, so marginal costs and prices respond less to the nominal wage. [Monetary policy](../concepts/monetary-policy.md) cannot condition on a flexibility that firms choose, because flexibility is a parameter. Source: [Optimal Monetary Policy in Production Networks](../sources/2022-05-01-lao-tahbaz-salehi-optimal-monetary-policy-production-networks.md).
- In Fang, Huo, Li, and Wu, attention and prices are a fixed point. If sector \(j\) learns more, its price moves more, downstream marginal costs become more volatile, and other sectors acquire more information. The wage rule changes that volatility, so policy changes \(\mu\). Stabilizing sectors that are already rigid further reduces their incentive to learn. Source: [Inattentive Network](../sources/2025-06-16-fang-huo-li-wu-inattentive-network.md).
- In Carvalho, Eusepi, Moench, and Preston, a belief that the inflation mean has risen leads firms to post higher prices, so inflation rises and the belief is partly confirmed. The strength of that loop is \(\Gamma\), which falls as [monetary policy](../concepts/monetary-policy.md) puts more weight on inflation and less on the output gap. Strict inflation targeting shuts the loop down. A measure-zero firm whose belief does not feed back into inflation would have revised its long-run expectation down faster after the early 1980s. Source: [Anchored Inflation Expectations](../sources/2023-01-01-carvalho-eusepi-moench-preston-anchored-inflation-expectations.md).
- In Flynn and Sastry, a higher \(\kappa\) shrinks the gap in output between an all-optimistic and an all-pessimistic economy. When strategic complementarity \(\omega\) is positive, that shrinkage itself rises with \(\kappa\). A more precise signal therefore removes the least output when narratives are already strong, because firms also use the narrative to forecast what other firms will do. The calibrated complementarity is \(\omega = 0.49\). Source: [The Macroeconomics of Narratives](../sources/2024-06-01-flynn-sastry-macroeconomics-of-narratives.md).

## Phenomena and puzzles explained

- La'O and Tahbaz-Salehi account for monetary nonneutrality that is stronger when any industry, including an upstream supplier, is stickier, and for an optimal price index that deviates from consumption shares. The information structure is an input to that result, calibrated from price-change frequencies. Source: [Optimal Monetary Policy in Production Networks](../sources/2022-05-01-lao-tahbaz-salehi-optimal-monetary-policy-production-networks.md).
- Carvalho, Eusepi, Moench, and Preston account for the rise of long-term US inflation expectations in the 1970s, the decline into the 1990s, and their stability through 2008. The model is estimated on short-term professional forecasts only. A constant gain throughout produces a counterfactual rise in the mid-2000s. Rational expectations and an exogenous inflation trend fit the held-out long-term surveys worse. The Michigan household series is predicted less well, and the late-1970s spike is missed. Source: [Anchored Inflation Expectations](../sources/2023-01-01-carvalho-eusepi-moench-preston-anchored-inflation-expectations.md).
- Flynn and Sastry account for hiring that rises with optimistic language while future productivity does not. Optimistic firms have lower subsequent stock returns and profitability, and their sales guidance exceeds realizations. At the estimated \(\kappa\), movements in the optimistic share still account for 19 percent of output variance. Source: [The Macroeconomics of Narratives](../sources/2024-06-01-flynn-sastry-macroeconomics-of-narratives.md).
- Fang, Huo, Li, and Wu account for EDGAR browsing. A 10 percentage-point higher input share is associated with 44 percent more browsing of that supplier, and a one-standard-deviation rise in the supplier's TFP volatility is associated with 14.5 percent more browsing. Attention falls with network distance. Total browsing rises with the variance of marginal-cost growth, which a fixed capacity does not predict. Greater browsing over the prior 90 days lines up with smaller earnings-per-share forecast errors. The hazard of browsing is increasing. A Calvo hazard is flat. The same model produces a positive association between sectoral shock volatility and the frequency of price changes. Source: [Inattentive Network](../sources/2025-06-16-fang-huo-li-wu-inattentive-network.md).
