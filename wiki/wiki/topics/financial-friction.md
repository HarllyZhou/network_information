# Financial friction

## How it is modelled

- Bordalo, Gennaioli, Shleifer, and Terry use one-period defaultable debt. The firm defaults when the value of repaying is below the value of defaulting. Lenders recover an exogenous fraction of depreciated capital and reprice the debt so that the diagnostically expected return equals the risk-free rate. The [credit spread](../concepts/credit-spread.md) is \(1/q - R\). This is not a collateral constraint. Source: [Real Credit Cycles](../sources/2026-10-16-bordalo-et-al-real-credit-cycle.md).

## Interaction with the real economy

- The [diagnostic expectation](../concepts/diagnostic-expectation.md) moves both sides of the debt market. Firms borrow and invest more when they are too optimistic. Lenders charge too little because they understate default. The reversal of those beliefs cuts investment and raises spreads together. Source: [Real Credit Cycles](../sources/2026-10-16-bordalo-et-al-real-credit-cycle.md).

## Phenomena and puzzles explained

- Predictable disappointment of earnings forecasts, low future bond returns after high investment and debt issuance, countercyclical credit spreads, and a large rise in spreads after a moderate TFP decline, as in 2007–2009. The same model without diagnosticity does not produce these movements. Source: [Real Credit Cycles](../sources/2026-10-16-bordalo-et-al-real-credit-cycle.md).
