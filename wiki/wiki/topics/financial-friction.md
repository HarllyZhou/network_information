# Financial friction

## How it is modelled

- Bordalo, Gennaioli, and Shleifer (2018). Firm \(\rho\) produces and repays if and only if the aggregate state satisfies \(\omega \ge \rho\), and defaults otherwise. The household prices that event at the diagnostic probability \(\mu\), so the expected return equals \(\beta^{-1}\). The [credit spread](../concepts/credit-spread.md) is \((1/\mu - 1)\beta^{-1}\). Default is not a value comparison, and there is no collateral constraint. Source: [Diagnostic Expectations and Credit Cycles](../sources/2018-02-01-bordalo-gennaioli-shleifer-diagnostic-expectations-credit-cycles.md).
- Bordalo, Gennaioli, Shleifer, and Terry (2026). One-period defaultable debt. The firm defaults when the value of repaying is below the value of defaulting. Lenders recover an exogenous fraction of depreciated capital and reprice the debt so that the diagnostically expected return equals the risk-free rate. The spread is \(1/q - R\). Source: [Real Credit Cycles](../sources/2026-10-16-bordalo-et-al-real-credit-cycle.md).

## Interaction with the real economy

- In the 2018 model, the firm and the household share a [diagnostic expectation](../concepts/diagnostic-expectation.md) of the aggregate state, and both use it as the perceived probability of repayment. That probability cancels from the firm's capital demand. A more optimistic household posts a lower contractual rate, the [credit spread](../concepts/credit-spread.md) falls, and the firm issues more debt and invests more. Source: [Diagnostic Expectations and Credit Cycles](../sources/2018-02-01-bordalo-gennaioli-shleifer-diagnostic-expectations-credit-cycles.md).
- In the 2026 model, the diagnostic expectation moves both sides of the debt market. Firms borrow and invest more when they are too optimistic about their own productivity. Lenders charge too little because they understate default. The reversal of those beliefs cuts investment and raises spreads together. Source: [Real Credit Cycles](../sources/2026-10-16-bordalo-et-al-real-credit-cycle.md).

## Phenomena and puzzles explained

- The 2018 model accounts for excess volatility of credit spreads, predictable errors and revisions in forecasts of spreads, a rise in the junk share when spreads are compressed, and a reversal of spreads and investment after good news. Rational expectations do not produce the reversal. Source: [Diagnostic Expectations and Credit Cycles](../sources/2018-02-01-bordalo-gennaioli-shleifer-diagnostic-expectations-credit-cycles.md).
- The 2026 model accounts for predictable disappointment of earnings forecasts, low future bond returns after high investment and debt issuance, countercyclical credit spreads, and a large rise in spreads after a moderate TFP decline, as in 2007–2009. The same model without diagnosticity does not produce these movements. Source: [Real Credit Cycles](../sources/2026-10-16-bordalo-et-al-real-credit-cycle.md).
