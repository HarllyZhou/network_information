# Carvalho, Eusepi, Moench, and Preston, Anchored Inflation Expectations (2023)

## Bibliographic

- Authors: Carlos Carvalho, Stefano Eusepi, Emanuel Moench, and Bruce Preston
- Venue: American Economic Journal: Macroeconomics 15 (1): 1–47
- Date: 2023-01
- Raw file: `raw/papers/2023-01-01-carvalho-eusepi-moench-preston-anchored-inflation-expectations.pdf`

## Key claims

1. Firms know the short-run law of motion and learn only the inflation mean. [Inflation expectation anchoring](../concepts/inflation-expectation-anchoring.md) is the decreasing-gain regime, in which long-run expectations become less sensitive to short-run surprises. The expectations are unanchored in the constant-gain regime, in which those surprises move the perceived mean one for one with the gain. Feeds [information friction](../topics/information-friction.md).
2. The mean is updated by \(\bar\pi_t = \bar\pi_{t-1} + k_t^{-1} f_{t-1}\). The gain falls with the sample size while a distance statistic built from the history of forecast errors stays below a threshold, and it jumps to a constant \(g\) otherwise. Feeds [information friction](../topics/information-friction.md).
3. [Monetary policy](../concepts/monetary-policy.md) is the targeting rule \(\pi_t - \gamma\pi_{t-1} + \lambda_x x_t = \phi_t\), optimal under discretion with a zero inflation target. Beliefs about the mean feed back into inflation with strength \(\Gamma\). Strict inflation targeting, \(\lambda_x \to 0\), shuts that feedback down. Feeds [optimal monetary policy](../topics/optimal-monetary-policy.md).
4. Estimated on CPI inflation and short-term professional forecasts only, 1955:I–2015:IV, the model predicts survey long-term expectations through the rise of the 1970s, the decline into the 1990s, and the stability of the 2000s, including 2008. Posterior means are about 2.5 percent for average inflation, 0.13 for indexation, 0.89 for \(\Gamma\), and 0.14 for the constant gain. A constant-gain-only model, rational expectations, and an exogenous inflation trend fit those long-term surveys worse. Feeds [information friction](../topics/information-friction.md) and [optimal monetary policy](../topics/optimal-monetary-policy.md).
