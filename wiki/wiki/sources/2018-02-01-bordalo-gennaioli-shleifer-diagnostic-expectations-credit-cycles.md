# Bordalo, Gennaioli, and Shleifer, Diagnostic Expectations and Credit Cycles (2018)

## Bibliographic

- Authors: Pedro Bordalo, Nicola Gennaioli, and Andrei Shleifer
- Venue: Journal of Finance 73 (1): 199–227
- Date: 2018-02
- Raw file: `raw/papers/2018-02-01-bordalo-gennaioli-shleifer-diagnostic-expectations-credit-cycles.pdf`

## Key claims

1. The firm and the household share a [diagnostic expectation](../concepts/diagnostic-expectation.md) of the aggregate state \(\omega_{t+1}\). For a normal AR(1), Proposition 1 gives \(\mathbb{E}_t^\theta(\omega_{t+1}) = \mathbb{E}_t(\omega_{t+1}) + \theta[\mathbb{E}_t(\omega_{t+1}) - \mathbb{E}_{t-1}(\omega_{t+1})]\). Feeds [credit cycle](../topics/credit-cycle.md).
2. Firm \(\rho\) produces and repays if and only if \(\omega_{t+1} \ge \rho\). The household prices that event at the diagnostic probability \(\mu(\rho, \mathbb{E}_t^\theta(\omega_{t+1}))\), so the expected return equals \(\beta^{-1}\). The [credit spread](../concepts/credit-spread.md) is \(S = (1/\mu - 1)\beta^{-1}\). The same probability cancels from the firm's capital demand, so investment falls in the spread. Feeds [financial friction](../topics/financial-friction.md).
3. Good news compresses spreads and raises investment. The overreaction then reverses, so the average spread is an ARMA(1,1). Under rational expectations it is only an AR(1). Forecast errors and revisions of credit spreads are predictable because households do not anticipate their own correction. Feeds [credit cycle](../topics/credit-cycle.md).
4. When optimism is high, riskier firms issue and invest more, so the junk share rises. That cross-section does not require diagnostic expectations. The predictable reversal of spreads does. Feeds [credit cycle](../topics/credit-cycle.md).
