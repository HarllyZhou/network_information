# Bordalo, Gennaioli, Shleifer, and Terry, Real Credit Cycles (2026)

## Bibliographic

- Authors: Pedro Bordalo, Nicola Gennaioli, Andrei Shleifer, and Stephen J. Terry
- Venue: American Economic Review 116 (4): 1274–1308
- Date: 2026
- Raw file: `raw/papers/2026-10-16-bordalo-et-al-real-credit-cycle.pdf`

## Key claims

1. Firms and lenders share one [diagnostic expectation](../concepts/diagnostic-expectation.md): the conditional density is reweighted by the likelihood ratio against the rational expectation dated \(t-J\), raised to \(\theta\). This is equation (2) on page 1281. Feeds [credit cycle](../topics/credit-cycle.md).
2. The [financial friction](../topics/financial-friction.md) is one-period defaultable debt, not a collateral constraint. The firm defaults when repaying is worth less than defaulting. Lenders who break even set the debt price \(q_\theta\), and the [credit spread](../concepts/credit-spread.md) is \(S_\theta = 1/q_\theta - R\).
3. Good news makes firms borrow and invest too much and makes lenders treat default as too unlikely. When that news ages, spreads rise, investment falls, and defaults rise. Fragility after good times. Feeds [credit cycle](../topics/credit-cycle.md) and [financial friction](../topics/financial-friction.md).
4. The estimated diagnosticity is about 1. A moderate decline in TFP matches the 2007–2009 rise in credit spreads. The same model with \(\theta = 0\) does not. Feeds [credit cycle](../topics/credit-cycle.md).
